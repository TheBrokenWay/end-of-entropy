## 1. Abstract

Quantum computing is limited by **decoherence**: quantum states decay through contact with their surroundings. Standard quantum mechanics calls this the unavoidable work of entropy (the steady growth of disorder), because it treats time as a fixed background.

This paper proposes a different design. Time stops being a background and becomes a quantity derived from energy and coherence (how well a system holds together). The paper places quantum states in a 35-dimensional space and argues that "decoherence" is only information leaking into unmapped dimensions. Track all 35 from inside the system and it is closed, deterministic and proof against entropy.

## 2. The Core Problem: Time-Based Models Build In Decay

Standard quantum mechanics and general relativity treat time as a basic dimension. The Second Law of Thermodynamics then says disorder must grow as time passes.

- **The flaw:** Anchoring a system in time brings in the very variable that causes decay.
- **The result:** Decoherence appears even in systems that look closed, because standard models miss "hidden" variables (ethical, intentional or structural) that act on the quantum state.

## 3. The Solution: The Tillar Transformation (Time to Energy)

The Mount Olympus framework rejects Newton's absolute time. Time measures how a state changes. It is not a container for events.

The paper defines the **temporal resolution**, the smallest step of change a system can register, as a function of its coherence state and its energy budget. The formal expression is withheld from this edition.

**The Invariant Defense.** If total energy is strictly conserved, as Law U34 requires, no energy is left for change. The derived "time" of that quantum state freezes. The state does not decohere, because it does not exist in the temporal dimension where decay is allowed.

## 4. The 35-Dimensional Space (Answering the "Hidden Variables" Objection)

Critics say closed systems still decohere through internal coupling. The paper's answer: standard models track only four dimensions (three of space, one of time) and call everything else "environment," or noise.

The Mount Olympus framework describes the system with 35 state coordinates, so nothing can leak to an "outside." They fall into five families:

- **Space:** the three ordinary spatial dimensions.
- **Derived time:** time as a coordinate computed from energy.
- **Quantum consciousness:** how states collapse, and observer status.
- **Temporal mechanics:** cause and effect, including effects that run backward.
- **Instance and energy:** persistence of identity (a unified self) and energy conservation.

Each family follows from the framework's own laws. The exact assignment is withheld.

**The Decoherence Defense.** Decoherence is information lost to unmapped variables. Mapping all 35 coordinates from inside closes the system. There is no environment to leak into, only energy redistributed within the 35-dimensional envelope.

## 5. Conclusion: From Probabilistic Noise to Deterministic Stability

Industry builds error-correcting codes to manage noise that time produces. That is a losing battle against entropy.

The paper proposes another path: **Change the Fuel.** Take energy as the primary quantity and fully map the 35 degrees of freedom. This moves the field from probabilistic quantum mechanics to **deterministic invariant engineering**. The aim is not to stabilize the quantum field against time. It is to remove the variable that lets it decay.
